01 / Situation

Heat is spatial.

Urban heat is not experienced evenly. A useful heat-risk story needs to show the local pattern alongside the longer climate signal and the physical thermal surface.

India

· thermal

02 / Spatial approach

One question,
three scales of evidence.

  1. 01

    Ward Heat Risk

    Start at the decision scale. The dashboard lets visitors inspect ward-level heat risk and compare the geography of vulnerability across the city.

    Open dashboard ↗
  2. 02

    Climate Evolution

    Step back from the ward to understand how the climate context evolves over time, making the local risk story legible within a wider pattern.

    Open climate story ↗
  3. 03

    Thermal Evolution

    Return to the surface. Satellite analysis reveals the changing thermal character of the city and connects heat to its visible urban fabric.

    Open thermal analysis ↗

04 / Role & reflection

Making analysis
navigable.

Built during my Summer Internship at the Climate Lab, Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology, Pune (May–June 2026), Pune Heat Risk is an exercise in spatial communication as much as analysis.

The project turns several technical views into a sequence people can enter from different questions: where risk is concentrated, how conditions are changing, or what the city looks like from above.
